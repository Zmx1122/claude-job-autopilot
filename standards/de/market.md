# Germany · How the market works for newcomers (career-coach reference)

Use this when advising someone with little or no job-search experience in Germany. State facts that change every year
(salary thresholds, permit rules) only after checking the official source; link it instead of quoting old numbers.

## Three gates
1. **CV gate** — the CV must fit the posting at a glance (recruiter and ATS). Without passing it nothing else matters.
2. **First interview** (often HR or the hiring manager, ~30 min) — convince in half an hour: motivation, fit, communication.
3. **Technical / case interview** — prove the skills in practice.
Coach for the gate the user is stuck at: no invitations → CV and targeting; invitations but no second round → interview prep.

## Language
- Many MINT master's programmes are taught in English, so international students often arrive without German.
  The job market does not follow: most Mittelstand teams and many corporate departments work in German day to day,
  and working-student jobs and internships often require it.
- English-only search works best for international tech companies, startups and research; outside that, German (B2–C1)
  multiplies the number of reachable postings. Recommend a concrete German plan whenever the user wants to stay.

## Choose the plan early (students)
- **Academia / PhD**: grades matter (aim for ≤ 2.0), find a professor's project early, write papers.
- **Return home after the degree**: finish on time at a well-ranked university; speed and reputation count most.
- **Work in Germany**: relevant working-student jobs and internships beat finishing in the standard period of study.
  Graduates with vertical experience (two related Werkstudent jobs, a thesis in industry) get hired; graduates with only
  grades struggle.

## Entry routes
| Route | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Werkstudent (working student) | enrolled students | ≤ 20 h/week in term; the most valuable experience for later hiring |
| Praktikum (internship) | students, sometimes graduates | mandatory internships are exempt from minimum wage; voluntary ones over 3 months are not |
| Abschlussarbeit (thesis in a company) | final-year students | often leads to an offer |
| Trainee programme | graduates | 12–24 months rotation, large companies |
| Junior / Einstieg / Absolvent | graduates | the normal entry-level job title keywords |

## Work permits (non-EU)
- Graduates of German universities can get a residence permit to look for a job after graduation (§ 20 AufenthG).
- EU Blue Card: salary thresholds change every year and are lower for shortage occupations and recent graduates —
  check make-it-in-germany.com before advising.
- Foreign degrees: check recognition in the anabin database (university "H+"); some regulated professions need formal recognition.
- Mention the permit status plainly in the cover letter; employers worry less when it is clear.

## Targeting
- Job hunting is not a supermarket: do not show everything you can do; deliver what this employer asked for.
- A personal portfolio page (projects, figures, talks, certificates) is the place for completeness — link it in the CV header.
- Track results: if fresh, tailored applications get more invitations than old ones (they usually do), shift effort there.
